Small Pots, Big Questions: 10 key answers on the pensions consolidation regime

Legal briefing28 September 20269 min readPensions

BehaviourReading time is calculated from the word count when the briefing is published. Dates always show the year. The bar under the prototype navigator shows reading progress.

Key points

  • There are around 13 million small, dormant pension pots, expected to rise to 20 million by 2030. The Government puts their value at over £4 billion and the cost of administering them at around £240 million a year.
  • The Government plans an automatic transfer process using multiple default consolidators, building on the Pension Schemes Act 2026.
  • The latest consultation covers which schemes and pots are in scope, what members are told, how consolidators are authorised and new duties for employers.
  • Implementation is expected from 2030, starting between April and June.

What to do now: master trusts and providers thinking of becoming consolidators should review the proposed authorisation criteria; DC schemes should check whether they are in scope and whether their data is ready; employers should start planning for the extra automatic enrolment duties.

Overview

The proliferation of small, dormant pots is an expensive and growing problem. The Government intends to solve it through an automatic transfer process, using multiple default consolidators, which will broadly require defined contribution schemes used for automatic enrolment to identify eligible pots, decide where each should be consolidated, and transfer it to an authorised small pot consolidator scheme.

1. Which DC schemes will fall in scope?

Broadly, defined contribution schemes used for automatic enrolment. The consultation sets out how the boundary would be drawn and what in-scope schemes would need to do with eligible pots.

2. Which pots will be eligible?

Pots that meet proposed tests for being small and dormant. Schemes will need to identify them from their own records, which is why data quality matters early.

3. Will any pots be excluded?

The Government is considering whether to exclude pots with guarantees, pots with protected pension ages and pots invested in Sharia-compliant funds.

4. What rights will members have?

Members must be told before a transfer, including their right to opt out or to choose a different consolidator.

5. What will authorised consolidators need to do?

Schemes that want to act as consolidators will need to meet authorisation criteria proposed by the Government. Master trusts and providers considering it should look at those criteria now.

6. Which consolidator receives the pot?

Where the member already has a pot with a consolidator scheme, that scheme. Otherwise, the proposed allocation rules decide where the pot goes.

7. What is the delivery model?

Transfers depend on shared digital infrastructure, and the start date is subject to a review of whether that infrastructure is ready.

8. Will TPR and the FCA have new powers?

Further detail is expected through the relevant TPR Codes of Practice and FCA rules, consulted on alongside the draft regulations.

9. What additional duties will employers have?

The proposals add to employers' automatic enrolment duties. Employers should start thinking about the processes they will need to comply.

10. What are the next steps?

A consultation on draft regulations, then a further consultation on ceding scheme duties, supervisory detail, final data standards and the remaining framework, planned for late 2027 or early 2028. The Government expects to implement the regime from 2030, starting between April and June.
